# Safety advice

**Version 1.0** — in force since {{document.effectiveAt}}

The vast majority of exchanges on Inbox go well. The few fraud attempts almost
always follow the same scenario, and can be recognised by a handful of signs.
Five minutes of reading can save you a great deal.

## 1. The four rules

**See the item before paying.** No photograph replaces an inspection on the
spot. An honest seller has no reason to refuse to let you see what you are
buying.

**Meet in a public, busy place.** In daylight. A market, a petrol station, the
entrance of a bank. Tell someone where you are going and who you are meeting.

**Never pay a deposit by Mobile Money to someone you have not met.** This is the
tipping point of nearly every fraud. A Mobile Money transfer is immediate and
practically irreversible.

**Stay on Inbox messaging.** As long as you are there, you keep a record of your
exchanges and you can report. As soon as you leave it, we can no longer do
anything for you.

## 2. Recognising a scam

These signs, above all when combined, should stop you:

- **A price that is too good.** A new phone at a third of its price does not
  exist.
- **A deposit demanded** before any meeting, to "reserve", "lock in the price"
  or "cover shipping costs".
- **The refusal of any meeting.** "I am travelling", "I am abroad", "my brother
  will hand it over to you".
- **Manufactured urgency.** "Several people are interested", "offer valid today
  only".
- **The invitation to leave Inbox** from the first messages onwards, to continue
  elsewhere.
- **A guaranteed return.** Doubled money, risk-free investment, quick gain. No
  serious investment promises a guaranteed return — ever.
- **Fees that appear afterwards**: customs, transport, unblocking, commission.
  Each payment calls for another.
- **Someone who refuses to be called** and only communicates in writing.
- **Payment by an unusual means**, or "in the name of a relative".

## 3. For buyers

- Inspect the item, switch it on, try it. For a phone, check that it is not
  blocked and ask for the invoice.
- For a vehicle: see the papers, check that they match the vehicle and the
  person in front of you.
- For accommodation: never advance a deposit before having visited and met the
  owner or their agent.
- Compare with other listings. An unexplained price gap is rarely explained in
  your favour.
- Pay at the moment of handover, not before.
- Be wary of an account created the day before, with no other listing and no
  history.

## 4. For sellers

- Only hand over the item once payment has been **actually received and
  verified** on your own account. A screenshot of a transfer proves nothing: one
  is fabricated in a minute.
- Be wary of a buyer who offers to pay **more than the asking price**, then asks
  you to send back the difference. This is a classic fraud.
- Never pass on a code received by SMS. **No legitimate buyer needs a code sent
  to your phone.** That code serves to take control of an account, not to pay.
- Your number is not displayed in the clear on your listings: visitors must
  explicitly ask to see it, and those requests are counted.
- For a handover at home, prefer a public place. If that is not possible, do not
  stay alone.

## 5. Protecting your account

- A password that is **long and specific to Inbox**. A four-word phrase is worth
  more than a short complicated word.
- **Nobody from Inbox will ever ask you for your password**, neither by message,
  nor by phone, nor by email.
- Check the address of the emails you receive. Ours come from {{site.domaine}}.
- Our emails never ask you to pass on a password, a code, or a card number.
- Review the list of your connected devices from your settings. An unknown
  device? Change your password: every session closes.
- **Write down the secret phrase backing up your messaging key.** Nobody can
  give it back to you, not even us.

## 6. What Inbox does, and cannot do

**What we do:**

- Every listing is checked by a person before it goes online.
- An automatic device spots known fraud wordings and places those listings at
  the head of the review queue.
- Reported accounts are reviewed, suspended or banned as the case requires.
- Your phone number stays hidden until you decide to show it.
- Your messages are encrypted end to end.

**What we cannot do:**

- We are **not a party to the transactions**. We do not hold the money, deliver
  nothing and refund nothing.
- We cannot **guarantee the honesty** of a member nor the condition of an item.
  An approved listing means that it complies with our rules, not that the seller
  is reliable.
- We cannot **read your messages** to settle a dispute: they are encrypted and we
  do not have the technical ability. If you want a conversation to be reviewed,
  you must agree to pass it on to us.
- We cannot **recover a payment** made outside Inbox.

## 7. Reporting

Every listing, every profile and every conversation carries a report button. You
may also write to **{{contact.abus}}**.

Report even if you have lost nothing: a listing withdrawn in time spares the
next person.

**If you have been the victim of a fraud:**

1. Stop all exchanges with the person and block them.
2. Report the conversation from Inbox. You will have to agree to pass the
   messages concerned on to us, your device decrypting them for us: we cannot
   read them otherwise.
3. Keep every piece of evidence: transfer references, screenshots, numbers used.
4. File a complaint with the competent authorities. Fraud is a criminal offence,
   and our moderation does not replace a complaint.
