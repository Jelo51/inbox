## 1. What may not be sold

The following list is not exhaustive. If in doubt, write to {{contact.general}}
before publishing.

### Weapons and explosives

Firearms, bladed weapons, ammunition, explosives, weapon parts, realistic
replicas, military equipment.

### Health products

Medicines, including over-the-counter ones. Food supplements presented as
treatments. Medical devices, injection equipment, corrective contact lenses.
Products presented as curing an illness.

### Narcotics and regulated substances

Drugs, related products, equipment intended for their consumption, psychotropic
substances, tobacco and alcohol sold without authorisation.

### Protected species and animal products

Animals of protected species, alive or dead. Ivory, pangolin scales, big-cat
skins, horns, hunting trophies. Animal parts obtained through poaching.

### Official documents and titles

Passports, national identity cards, driving licences, civil status records,
diplomas, certificates, professional cards, tickets, named travel documents —
whether genuine or forged.

### Counterfeits

Copies of branded products, whatever their quality. Wordings such as "first
copy", "replica" or "perfect imitation" signal a counterfeit.

### Stolen goods

Any item you are not the lawful owner of. Phones that are blocked or reported
stolen.

### Sexual content

Sexual services, pornographic content, sexually explicit objects. Any listing
referring to minors in this context is reported to the authorities.

### Financial services and investments

Peer-to-peer lending, currency exchange services, money transfers, credit
operations without authorisation. **Any investment promising a guaranteed
return**, "doubled money", a quick gain or multiplied capital. Lotteries,
tontines with promised returns, pyramid recruitment schemes.

These listings are rejected systematically. They are the costliest of all for
their victims.

### Personal data

Contact files, phone number databases, email address lists, user accounts of
third-party services.

### Miscellaneous

Human organs and body products. Illegal services. Objects diverted from their
purpose in order to commit an offence. Undeclared work.

## 2. What is forbidden in the wording

- **Requiring a deposit before any meeting or inspection.** This is the most
  frequent fraud pattern.
- **Refusing any meeting or any trial** of the item before payment.
- Advertising a price plainly inconsistent with the item described, in order to
  attract contacts.
- Publishing the same listing several times to occupy the results.
- Putting content unrelated to the item in the description, in order to appear
  in more searches.
- Passing yourself off as a business, an administration or a person you are not.
- Inviting the other party, from the outset, to leave Inbox messaging and
  continue elsewhere: that is where we can no longer do anything for you.
- Publishing a third party's contact details without their agreement.
- Using insulting, discriminatory, hateful or threatening language.

## 3. Writing a good listing

What makes a listing succeed:

- **A precise title**: brand, model, capacity, condition. "iPhone 13 Pro 128 GB,
  very good condition" is worth more than "phone for sale".
- **An honest description.** Mention the defects. A buyer who discovers a defect
  on the spot walks away; a forewarned buyer comes with the right price in mind.
- **The right price, in CFA francs, without decimals.** State whether the price
  is negotiable.
- **The right category and the right city.** Wrong filing deprives you of the
  buyers who were looking for you.
- **The neighbourhood**, if you accept handover in person.

## 4. Photographs

- From **one to eight photographs**, 8 MB maximum each, in JPEG, PNG or WebP
  format.
- The photographs must show **the item actually offered**. No catalogue image in
  place of the object.
- No photograph containing a phone number, an address or an advertising
  watermark.
- No identifiable person without their agreement.

**The metadata of your photographs is removed automatically** before
publication, including the GPS coordinates recorded by your device. A photo
taken at your home will not broadcast your address.

## 5. Grounds for rejection

A rejection always carries one of the following grounds, which is communicated
to you:

| Ground                             | What it means                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Prohibited product or service      | The item appears in section 1                        |
| Suspected counterfeit              | Serious indications of a copied brand                |
| Misleading or inconsistent price   | Price unrelated to the item described                |
| Unsuitable category                | The listing is not in the right section              |
| Unusable photos                    | Photos missing, illegible or unrelated               |
| Duplicate listing                  | The same item is already published                   |
| Contact details in the description | Phone or address in the text                         |
| Suspected fraud attempt            | Deposit required, meeting refused, promised gain     |
| Adults-only content                | Sexual content                                       |
| Other                              | A breach not covered above, specified in the message |

## 6. What happens after a rejection

You receive an email with the ground and, where applicable, a clarification
from the team.

You may correct your listing and submit it again: **a rejected listing does not
consume any quota**. We do not charge you for a decision that is not yours.

If you believe the rejection is a mistake, write to {{contact.general}} quoting
the listing reference.

## 7. Penalties

Depending on seriousness and repetition:

- **Rejection of the listing**, with its ground.
- **Removal of a listing already online**, where the breach is found after
  publication.
- **Temporary suspension of the account**, for a determined and reasoned
  duration.
- **Permanent ban**, in the event of a serious or repeated breach — in
  particular an attempted fraud, the sale of prohibited products, identity
  usurpation, or the creation of a new account to circumvent a penalty.

Every decision is reasoned and communicated to you. Moderation decisions are
recorded in an unalterable internal log.

A professional subscription running at the time of a ban for a serious breach
does not give rise to any refund.
